# Layout

## Pages

- Every page someone can open is in the sidebar, always open beside the page on a laptop. On a phone, the first four
  are tabs along the bottom, and More opens the same sidebar as a sheet. Support and the account menu sit at the
  sidebar's foot.
- A page has a title and no subtitle. Its status, such as a plan's Draft badge, sits beside the title, at the title's
  size, not among the buttons.
- Actions go on the right, where a thumb reaches them on a phone.
- Sections are stacked, one under the next, with room between them, and each table is full width.
- Two tables go side by side, each half width, only when both have one or two columns of values with short names, and
  they belong together, such as Rates and Payroll taxes (`SectionPair`). A small table with no partner stays full
  width, and two unrelated ones aren't paired just because they fit.
- Headings follow the page: a page's sections are its top headings. Don't wrap every section in one heading such as
  "Rates".
- Underline tabs (`TabsList variant="line"`) for named sections.

## Showing and hiding

- Don't hide what people read every time behind a toggle or a collapsed section. `CollapsibleSection` is for what's
  checked now and then, such as assumptions, and once opened it shows everything inside, with no second
  toggle.
- An account with one line doesn't open to show that line again.

## Tables

- Every table has a default sort that makes sense for it: offerings by start date, money by size, high to low, and
  alphabetical when nothing else fits.
- A chart-of-accounts number sits in its own column on the left, before the account's name, so numbers and names line
  up. Everywhere an amount maps to an account, show it the way the program area pages do (`AccountTable`).
- Numbers are right-aligned in tabular figures, with totals in the table footer. A group of rows that adds up gets a
  subtotal. A table that doesn't add up, such as fees, gets none.
- One kind of unit to a table: dollars, or counts, or days, not a mix.
- A yes-or-no value, in a table or anywhere on a page, is a read-only checkbox (`BooleanCheck`), checked or cleared.
  Never the words Yes, No, On, Off, True, or False, and never a blank for no.
- Where revenue, costs, and net appear, every step between them is on the page, so the reader can add it up.
- Headers don't wrap. Shorten the words, or drop a column whose value is the same in every row.
- A table too wide for a phone scrolls inside its card instead of widening the page.

## Lists and forms

- An inline "Add \{thing\}" row goes at the top of a list, never below it.
- One way to save a form: explicit Save and Discard, with a warning before leaving unsaved changes.
- Edit a value where it's shown, in its own column, when that's simple. Otherwise, a row's actions go in a menu behind
  a kebab button at the row's end.
- A list of short notes, such as a plan's, is a real list, one note to a bullet (`RulesList`).

## Icons, charts, and color

- An icon-only button has an `aria-label` and a tooltip with the same name, and each action keeps one icon everywhere.
- A chart sits beside the table of the same numbers, never on its own.
- A color means one thing. Don't shade it darker to mean "more"; use one consistent color and say the number.
